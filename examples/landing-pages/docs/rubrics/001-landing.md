# RUBRIC-001: Landing page

A landing page that sells one B2B SaaS product to a reader who signs up on
their own, with no sales call. The criteria follow Harry Dry's guide,
"My step-by-step guide to landing pages that convert"
(https://marketingexamples.com/landing-page/guide), and his
"17 tips for great copywriting" (https://marketingexamples.com/copywriting/tips).
The Sources list below names the step or tip each criterion comes from. Two choices are the
owner's, not Harry's: the price is on the page, because the buyer is
self-serve, and proof is required, because the page is meant to sell.

Each criterion is one decidable claim and says what is exempt. Sentence
length and the banned-word check are ctxgrd's, and a check a regex can
decide is not here. A hint may name words to look at.
The guide's second call to action, FAQ, and founder's note are folded into
C-004, C-007, and C-003. With four should criteria, the genre sets its
threshold to 0.75 so that one should miss warns instead of blocking.

Revised on 2026-09-06 after four critiques, two by Lanham's Paramedic
Method and two by Gopen and Swan's reader-expectation method: each fail
condition now follows "fails when" instead of sitting inside a long
subject, and each permission has its own sentence. The sources moved out of
the statements the same day: the judge applies whatever a statement says,
and a label pins to the statement's hash, so a citation edit must never
orphan a case.

- C-006: guide step 7, make the value concrete; tip 3, no one cares what you can do.
- C-007: guide steps 7 and 9, objections and tie up loose ends; tip 15, fence sitters don't buy.

- C-009: the guide's core test: would this help me sell if I met the customer in person? If not, remove it.

## Criteria

- **C-001** (must): The title names, in the reader's terms, what the product does and the value the reader gets from it. A visitor who reads only the title knows what is sold. A title fails when it carries a value with no product, such as a slogan or a motto. A title fails when it carries a product with no value, such as a category name.
  - hint: the first heading on the page, read on its own without the line under it
- **C-002** (must): The subtitle says how the product creates the value it sells. It names the mechanism, the thing the product does. It does not restate the title in other words.
  - hint: the line directly under the title
- **C-003** (must): Every proof point names a person with role and company, a named customer, or a counted figure. No praise of the product stands without one. A founder's note in the first person is exempt.
  - hint: testimonials, logos, ratings, counts of customers or reviews, and any sentence that says the product is loved, trusted, or used
- **C-004** (must): Every call to action on the page leads to the same next step. One label may repeat down the page. Each button label names the thing the reader gets. It may open with a verb such as get or start. A label fails when it is only a generic verb, such as submit, learn more, or get started. A secondary link to pricing or docs is exempt.
  - hint: every button, and every link styled as one
- **C-005** (must): The page states the price, the free tier, or the trial terms in its own text. A page that links to a pricing page instead fails.
  - hint: the pricing block, and the text beside each call to action
- **C-006** (should): Each feature section states what the reader gets from the feature and ties it back to the title's promise. A section fails when it names only a capability and puts no reader in the sentence.
  - hint: each feature heading and the paragraph under it
- **C-007** (should): The page answers the reader's largest objections in words a customer would use. The answers sit in the features, in an FAQ, or beside a call to action. An objection the page leaves to a sales call fails.
  - hint: the feature sections, the FAQ, and the text under each button
- **C-008** (should): Every claim on the page is a fact the reader could check or a plain description of what the product does. A sentence fails when an adjective, an adverb, or a superlative carries its meaning.
  - hint: every sentence about the product, then its adjectives, adverbs, and words such as seamless, powerful, effortless, best
- **C-009** (should): Every section would help sell if the writer said it to the customer face to face. A slogan, a stock claim, or a section that repeats another one fails.
  - hint: each section, taken one at a time
